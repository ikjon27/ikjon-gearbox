MCL sprain: rehab exercises & recovery plan

The ligament on the inner side of the knee. Injured by a blow to the outside of the knee or the knee caving inward. Grades 1–2 heal well without surgery.

Common in: Football, wrestling, skiing · Body area: Knee · Updated November 2026

Written by: IKJON teamSources: 1 studies and guidelinesLast checked: 9 November 2026How we write health pages →

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Understand the injury

What's injured

The MCL (medial collateral ligament) is a strap on the inner side of the knee. It stops the knee bending inward.

How it happens

A hit to the outside of the knee (a tackle) or the knee collapsing inward when landing or skiing.

How serious can it be?

Grade 1

Stretched — tender on the inner knee but stable. Usually 1–3 weeks.

Grade 2

Partly torn — some looseness and pain bending. Usually 3–6 weeks, sometimes with a brace.

Grade 3

Fully torn — the knee opens on the inner side. Often happens with an ACL tear; see a doctor.

How it heals

The MCL has a good blood supply, so it usually heals without surgery. Gentle movement and progressive strength help the new fibres line up strongly.

Muscles to strengthen — recover and come back stronger

  • QuadricepsSupport the knee and restore full straightening.
  • Adductors (inner thigh)The inner-thigh muscles sit right next to the MCL and help protect the inner knee.
  • Gluteus medius (side hip)Stops the knee collapsing inward — the movement that injures the MCL.
  • HamstringsAdd stability on the inner and back of the knee.

Come back stronger

  • Return to side-to-side movement gradually: walk, jog, then cut.
  • Strengthen the inner thigh (adductors) and side hip twice a week.
  • Use a hinged brace for contact sport only if your doctor or physio advises it.

Typical signs

  • Pain on the inner side of the knee
  • Pain when the knee is pushed inward

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • Knee feels very loose
  • Big swelling within hours (may be ACL too)

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Weeks 0–1

Protect

Goal: Movement and quad activation.

  1. Quad sets3 × 10

    Isometric · Quadriceps activation · 5 s hold
    1. Sit or lie with the leg straight and a rolled towel under the knee.
    2. Tighten the thigh to press the back of the knee into the towel.
    3. Hold, then relax fully.

    Tip: Watch the kneecap glide up as the muscle switches on.

  2. Straight leg raise3 × 10

    Strength · Quadriceps, hip flexors
    1. Lie on your back, other knee bent.
    2. Lock the straight knee with a quad set first.
    3. Lift the leg to the height of the other knee, lower slowly.

    Tip: If the knee bends during the lift (a 'lag'), go back to quad sets.

  3. Heel slides3 × 15

    Mobility · Knee bending range
    1. Lie on your back.
    2. Slide the heel towards your bottom as far as comfortable.
    3. Slide back to straight.

    Tip: Use a towel around the foot to help if needed.

  4. Stationary bike10 min

    Cardio · Knee range, fitness
    1. Seat high enough that the knee is almost straight at the bottom.
    2. Low resistance.
    3. Steady pedalling.

    Tip: Rock back and forth first if a full turn is not yet possible.

Move on when:

  • Walking without limp
Stage 2 · Weeks 1–4

Strengthen

Goal: Straight-line strength and balance.

  1. Mini squat (0–45°)3 × 12

    Strength · Quads, glutes
    1. Feet hip-width, hold a support if needed.
    2. Sit back a little as if to a high chair, knees to about 45°.
    3. Push through both heels to stand.

    Tip: Knees track over the 2nd toe, not caving in.

  2. Step-ups3 × 10

    Strength · Quads, glutes
    1. Face a low step (10–20 cm).
    2. Step up with the working leg and stand tall.
    3. Step down slowly with the other leg.

    Tip: Raise the step height as strength improves.

  3. Terminal knee extension3 × 15

    Strength · Vastus medialis, last degrees of extension
    1. Band around the back of the knee, anchored in front.
    2. Start with the knee slightly bent.
    3. Push the knee back to fully straight against the band, squeeze 2 s.

    Tip: Slow and controlled, no snapping into lock.

  4. Glute bridge3 × 12

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
    2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips into a straight line.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Push through heels; don't arch the low back.

  5. Single-leg balance3 × 30 s

    Balance · Balance, ankle reflexes
    1. Stand on one leg, knee soft.
    2. Progress: eyes closed → on a pillow → catching a ball.
    3. Stay tall.

    Tip: Balance training reduces repeat ankle sprains.

  6. Side-lying hip abduction3 × 12

    Strength · Glute medius
    1. Lie on your side, bottom knee bent.
    2. Lift the straight top leg slightly behind the body.
    3. Lower slowly, toes pointing forward.

    Tip: Strong hip abductors reduce knee 'caving in'.

  7. Seated knee extension3 × 12

    Strength · Quadriceps
    1. Sit tall on a chair.
    2. Straighten one knee fully and squeeze the thigh for 2 s.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Add an ankle weight to progress. After ACL surgery, ask your surgeon when this is allowed.

Move on when:

  • Full range, no pain on inner knee during squats
Stage 3 · Weeks 3–6

Side-to-side

Goal: Lateral strength and cutting.

  1. Split squat3 × 8

    Strength · Quads, glutes, balance
    1. Long stride stance, back heel up.
    2. Lower the back knee straight down towards the floor.
    3. Drive up through the front heel.

    Tip: Front knee may go over the toes; keep it in line with the foot.

  2. Lateral lunge3 × 8

    Strength · Adductors, glutes
    1. Wide stance.
    2. Shift to one side, bending that knee, other leg straight.
    3. Push back to centre.

    Tip: Keep the bent knee in line with the foot.

  3. Forward step-down3 × 10

    Strength · Eccentric quad control
    1. Stand on a step on the working leg.
    2. Slowly lower the other heel to touch the floor in front.
    3. Push back up without locking the knee hard.

    Tip: Knee stays in line with the foot. Use a mirror.

  4. Lateral bounds3 × 6

    Plyometric · Side-to-side power & control
    1. Jump sideways from one leg to the other.
    2. Land and stick for 2 s before the next.
    3. Increase distance gradually.

    Tip: Knee must not cave inward on landing.

  5. Agility & cutting drills4 × 20 s

    Plyometric · Sport-specific movement
    1. Ladder drills, then cone shuttles.
    2. Planned cuts at 45°, later 90°.
    3. Finally unplanned cuts reacting to a partner's signal.

    Tip: Unplanned cutting is the last step before full training.

Move on when:

  • Cutting and lateral bounds without pain

Important: Avoid side-to-side stress early. A hinged brace may be used for grade 2–3.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Moderate

  • Most grade I–II MCL sprains heal without surgery with a period of protection (sometimes a hinged brace) and progressive strengthening.
  • This is based on clinical practice and cohort studies rather than large randomised trials.

Sources: Clinical practice consensus and cohort studies

Common questions

Can you walk with an MCL sprain?

Yes, most people with a mild or moderate MCL sprain can walk, sometimes with a brace for the first weeks. Avoid twisting and side-stepping movements until the knee feels stable.

Does an MCL tear need surgery?

Rarely. The MCL has a good blood supply, so even many complete tears heal with a brace and rehab. Surgery is mainly considered when other ligaments are also torn.

When can I return to football after an MCL sprain?

Typically after about 3–6 weeks for a moderate sprain and longer for a severe one. You should be able to run, cut, hop and kick without pain and with no feeling of the knee opening on the inside.

How long does mcl sprain take to heal?

It depends on severity. Mild: about 30 days (about 4 weeks). Moderate: about 45 days (about 6 weeks). Severe: about 90 days (about 3 months). Move on by meeting each stage's checks, not by the calendar alone.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: Knee feels very loose; Big swelling within hours (may be ACL too).

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
