Hamstring strain: rehab exercises & recovery plan

A tear in the muscles at the back of the thigh, usually during sprinting or kicking. The most common injury in football, and it often comes back if rehab is rushed.

Common in: Football, sprinting · Body area: Thigh · Updated November 2026

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Typical signs

  • Sudden sharp pain at the back of the thigh while sprinting
  • Pain bending the knee against resistance
  • Bruising

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • Pop near the sitting bone with large bruising (possible tendon tear)
  • Cannot walk

It depends on how serious the injury is. These are typical plan lengths:

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Days 0–7

Protect

Goal: Gentle activation, walk normally.

  1. Isometric heel dig5 × 10 s

    Isometric · Hamstrings (early) · 30–50% effort
    1. Lie on your back, knee bent at about 30°.
    2. Dig the heel into the floor at low effort (30–50%).
    3. Hold, then relax.

    Tip: Should feel like work, not pain.

  2. Glute bridge3 × 10

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
    2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips into a straight line.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Push through heels; don't arch the low back.

  3. Brisk walk15 min

    Cardio · Fitness, tissue health
    1. Walk on flat ground at a pace that raises breathing.
    2. Keep posture tall and arms swinging.

    Tip: For low back pain, walking is one of the best treatments.

  4. Stationary bike10 min

    Cardio · Knee range, fitness
    1. Seat high enough that the knee is almost straight at the bottom.
    2. Low resistance.
    3. Steady pedalling.

    Tip: Rock back and forth first if a full turn is not yet possible.

Move on when:

  • Normal walking
  • Pain-free isometric at full effort
Stage 2 · Weeks 1–3

Strength

Goal: Hamstring strength through range.

  1. Single-leg bridge3 × 10

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Bridge position, lift one foot off the floor.
    2. Raise hips level, not twisting.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Hips stay level like a table.

  2. Slider hamstring curl3 × 8

    Strength · Hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, heels on towels on a smooth floor.
    2. Lift hips into a bridge.
    3. Slide the heels out slowly, then pull them back in.

    Tip: Start with two legs, progress to one.

  3. Hip hinge drill3 × 10

    Mobility · Movement pattern
    1. Hold a stick along your spine touching head, upper back and tailbone.
    2. Push the hips back, keeping all 3 contact points.
    3. Return by driving hips forward.

    Tip: The safe way to bend and lift.

  4. Single-leg Romanian deadlift3 × 8

    Strength · Hamstrings, glutes, balance
    1. Stand on one leg with a soft knee.
    2. Hinge forward, the free leg going back, back flat.
    3. Return to standing by squeezing the glute.

    Tip: Hold a wall at first. Hips stay square to the floor.

  5. Walk–jog intervals15 min

    Cardio · Return to running
    1. Start: 1 min jog / 1 min walk × 10.
    2. Each pain-free session, add jog time.
    3. Target 20–30 min continuous easy running.

    Tip: Run on flat, even ground first.

  6. Bridge march3 × 10

    Core · Glutes, core
    1. Lift into a bridge.
    2. Lift one foot a little off the floor, then the other.
    3. Keep the hips level the whole time.

    Tip: If the hips drop, go back to normal bridges.

Move on when:

  • Pain-free single-leg bridge ×20
  • Jogging pain-free
Stage 3 · Weeks 3–6

Speed & eccentric

Goal: Lengthening strength and faster running.

  1. Nordic hamstring curl3 × 5

    Strength · Eccentric hamstrings
    1. Kneel with a partner holding your ankles.
    2. Lower your body forward as slowly as possible, hips straight.
    3. Catch yourself with your hands, push back up.

    Tip: One of the best-proven hamstring injury preventers in football.

  2. Rear-foot elevated split squat3 × 8

    Strength · Single-leg strength
    1. Back foot on a bench behind you.
    2. Lower down on the front leg under control.
    3. Drive up through the front foot.

    Tip: Add dumbbells or a backpack when 3×10 is easy.

  3. Running strides6 × 1

    Cardio · Speed, hamstring loading · 60→80% speed
    1. Run 60–80 m, building up gradually.
    2. Reach the target speed in the middle third.
    3. Walk back to recover.

    Tip: Progress 60% → 75% → 90% → 100% effort across sessions.

  4. Single-leg Romanian deadlift3 × 10

    Strength · Hamstrings, glutes, balance · loaded
    1. Stand on one leg with a soft knee.
    2. Hinge forward, the free leg going back, back flat.
    3. Return to standing by squeezing the glute.

    Tip: Hold a wall at first. Hips stay square to the floor.

Move on when:

  • Strides at 90% without pain
Stage 4 · Week 6+

Return to play

Goal: Full sprint, kicking, team training.

  1. Running strides6 × 1

    Cardio · Speed, hamstring loading · build to 100%
    1. Run 60–80 m, building up gradually.
    2. Reach the target speed in the middle third.
    3. Walk back to recover.

    Tip: Progress 60% → 75% → 90% → 100% effort across sessions.

  2. Agility & cutting drills4 × 20 s

    Plyometric · Sport-specific movement
    1. Ladder drills, then cone shuttles.
    2. Planned cuts at 45°, later 90°.
    3. Finally unplanned cuts reacting to a partner's signal.

    Tip: Unplanned cutting is the last step before full training.

  3. Nordic hamstring curl3 × 6

    Strength · Eccentric hamstrings · keep 1–2×/week
    1. Kneel with a partner holding your ankles.
    2. Lower your body forward as slowly as possible, hips straight.
    3. Catch yourself with your hands, push back up.

    Tip: One of the best-proven hamstring injury preventers in football.

Move on when:

  • Full-speed sprint pain-free
  • Strength 90%+ of other leg
  • Full team training

Important: Re-injury rates are high. Nordic curls and full-speed sprinting before return are the most important prevention steps.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Strong

  • A rehab programme focused on lengthening exercises got footballers back to play in about 28 days, compared with 51 days for conventional exercises.
  • Programmes that include the Nordic hamstring exercise cut hamstring injuries by about half.

Sources: Askling et al. 2013, British Journal of Sports Medicine (RCT); van Dyk et al. 2019, British Journal of Sports Medicine (meta-analysis of 8,459 athletes)

Common questions

How long does hamstring strain take to heal?

It depends on severity. Mild: about 30 days (about 4 weeks). Moderate: about 60 days (about 9 weeks). Severe: about 90 days (about 3 months). Move on by meeting each stage's checks, not by the calendar alone.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: Pop near the sitting bone with large bruising (possible tendon tear); Cannot walk.

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
