Stress fracture in the low back (spondylolysis): rehab exercises & recovery plan

A stress fracture in a small part of a lower vertebra, caused by repeated bending backwards and twisting. It is the most common cause of ongoing back pain in young athletes. It needs a doctor's diagnosis (usually a scan), and most heal with rest from sport and rehab.

Common in: Teen footballers, gymnasts, cricket bowlers, wrestlers · Body area: Back & core · Updated November 2026

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Understand the injury

What's injured

A stress fracture in a small bridge of bone at the back of a lower vertebra (the pars), most often L5. It is the most common cause of ongoing back pain in young athletes.

How it happens

Repeated bending backwards and twisting — fast bowling, gymnastics, football kicking, wrestling — during growth.

How serious can it be?

Stress reaction

Caught early, before a full crack.

Confirmed fracture

Crack seen on a scan — typical 3–4 month programme.

Long-standing

Pain for over 6 months — slower return.

How it heals

With a break from bending backwards and twisting, most heal or settle within 3–6 months. A gradual, supervised return keeps it from coming back.

Muscles to strengthen — recover and come back stronger

  • Core (abs & obliques)Holds the spine steady so the pars is protected.
  • GlutesPower for kicking and bowling without arching the back.
  • HamstringsTight hamstrings increase strain on the low back.
  • Hip flexorsTight hip flexors tilt the pelvis and arch the back.

Come back stronger

  • Avoid back-arching moves until your doctor clears you.
  • Build core endurance (planks, dead bugs) before sport.
  • Limit bowling or high-load repetitions during growth spurts.

Start the Stress fracture in the low back plan

Typical signs

  • Low back pain in an athlete aged 10–20
  • Worse bending backwards or twisting (kicking, bowling, back walkovers)
  • Often on one side

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • Suspected? Stop sport and see a doctor for a scan before starting
  • Numbness or weakness in the legs
  • Pain at night or at rest

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Weeks 0–6

Relative rest

Goal: No sport; let the bone heal with gentle core work.

  1. Brisk walk20 min

    Cardio · Fitness, tissue health
    1. Walk on flat ground at a pace that raises breathing.
    2. Keep posture tall and arms swinging.

    Tip: For low back pain, walking is one of the best treatments.

  2. Dead bug3 × 8

    Core · Deep core control
    1. On your back, arms up, hips and knees at 90°.
    2. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly.
    3. Keep the low back gently against the floor.

    Tip: Breathe out as the limbs lower.

  3. Glute bridge3 × 10

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
    2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips into a straight line.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Push through heels; don't arch the low back.

  4. Side plank3 × 15 s

    Core · Obliques, side of the trunk · knees bent
    1. On your side, elbow under the shoulder.
    2. Knees bent first, then straight legs.
    3. Lift hips into a line and hold.

    Tip: Head, hips, feet in one line.

  5. Hamstring stretch2 × 30 s

    Stretch · Back of thigh
    1. Lie on your back with a towel around one foot.
    2. Lift the straight leg until you feel a stretch.
    3. Hold, relaxing the leg.

    Tip: Keep the other leg flat or bent — whatever is comfortable.

  6. Hip flexor stretch2 × 30 s

    Stretch · Hip front flexibility
    1. Half-kneel, back knee on a cushion.
    2. Tuck the pelvis under and squeeze the back glute.
    3. Shift forward gently.

    Tip: Breathe slowly; no bouncing.

Move on when:

  • Pain-free in daily life
  • Doctor happy with healing
Stage 2 · Weeks 6–10

Core control

Goal: Trunk control without backward bending.

  1. Bird dog3 × 8

    Core · Back extensors, core
    1. On hands and knees.
    2. Reach opposite arm and leg out long.
    3. Hold 5–10 s, don't rotate the hips.

    Tip: Imagine a cup of tea on your lower back.

  2. Side plank3 × 30 s

    Core · Obliques, side of the trunk
    1. On your side, elbow under the shoulder.
    2. Knees bent first, then straight legs.
    3. Lift hips into a line and hold.

    Tip: Head, hips, feet in one line.

  3. Pallof press3 × 10

    Core · Core anti-rotation
    1. Stand side-on to a band anchored at chest height.
    2. Hold the band at your chest, then press it straight out.
    3. Hold 2 s without twisting, bring it back.

    Tip: The trunk must not rotate towards the anchor.

  4. Bridge march3 × 10

    Core · Glutes, core
    1. Lift into a bridge.
    2. Lift one foot a little off the floor, then the other.
    3. Keep the hips level the whole time.

    Tip: If the hips drop, go back to normal bridges.

  5. Dead bug3 × 10

    Core · Deep core control
    1. On your back, arms up, hips and knees at 90°.
    2. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly.
    3. Keep the low back gently against the floor.

    Tip: Breathe out as the limbs lower.

Move on when:

  • Side plank 45 s each side pain-free
Stage 3 · Weeks 10–13

Strength

Goal: Full-body strength.

  1. Squat3 × 10

    Strength · Quads, glutes
    1. Feet shoulder-width (hold a weight at the chest to progress).
    2. Sit down between the heels to a pain-free depth.
    3. Stand tall, squeezing glutes.

    Tip: 3 s down, 3 s up builds strength in tendons too.

  2. Split squat3 × 8

    Strength · Quads, glutes, balance
    1. Long stride stance, back heel up.
    2. Lower the back knee straight down towards the floor.
    3. Drive up through the front heel.

    Tip: Front knee may go over the toes; keep it in line with the foot.

  3. Kettlebell deadlift3 × 8

    Strength · Hips, back strength
    1. Weight between the feet.
    2. Hinge at the hips, flat back, grab the handle.
    3. Stand by driving hips forward.

    Tip: Gradual loading builds a stronger, less sensitive back.

  4. Suitcase carry3 × 30 s

    Core · Core anti-side-bend
    1. Hold a heavy weight in one hand.
    2. Walk tall for 20–30 m without leaning.
    3. Switch hands.

    Tip: Shoulders level the whole time.

  5. Single-leg Romanian deadlift3 × 8

    Strength · Hamstrings, glutes, balance
    1. Stand on one leg with a soft knee.
    2. Hinge forward, the free leg going back, back flat.
    3. Return to standing by squeezing the glute.

    Tip: Hold a wall at first. Hips stay square to the floor.

Move on when:

  • Lifting and jogging pain-free
Stage 4 · Week 13+

Return to sport

Goal: Running, then sport-specific moves.

  1. Walk–jog intervals20 min

    Cardio · Return to running
    1. Start: 1 min jog / 1 min walk × 10.
    2. Each pain-free session, add jog time.
    3. Target 20–30 min continuous easy running.

    Tip: Run on flat, even ground first.

  2. Skipping (A-skips)3 × 20

    Cardio · Running coordination, calf spring
    1. Skip forward driving one knee up.
    2. Land on the ball of the foot.
    3. Swing the arms opposite to the legs.

    Tip: Great warm-up before running and sprinting.

  3. Agility & cutting drills4 × 20 s

    Plyometric · Sport-specific movement
    1. Ladder drills, then cone shuttles.
    2. Planned cuts at 45°, later 90°.
    3. Finally unplanned cuts reacting to a partner's signal.

    Tip: Unplanned cutting is the last step before full training.

  4. Box jump3 × 5

    Plyometric · Power, landing
    1. Stand in front of a low box.
    2. Swing the arms and jump onto the box, landing softly.
    3. Step (don't jump) back down.

    Tip: Land in a quiet half-squat, knees out.

  5. Side plank3 × 45 s

    Core · Obliques, side of the trunk
    1. On your side, elbow under the shoulder.
    2. Knees bent first, then straight legs.
    3. Lift hips into a line and hold.

    Tip: Head, hips, feet in one line.

Move on when:

  • Full training pain-free
  • Cleared by doctor

Important: Avoid backward bending and twisting sport moves until your doctor clears you. Phase 1 is relative rest — everyday activity is fine.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Moderate

  • About 92% of young athletes with this stress fracture returned to sport with non-surgical treatment (rest from sport plus rehab) — slightly more than after surgery.
  • Early diagnosis with a scan and rest from backward-bending sport moves are key.

Sources: Overley et al. 2017, Global Spine Journal (meta-analysis, 376 patients)

Common questions

How long does stress fracture in the low back take to heal?

It depends on severity. Stress reaction: about 90 days (about 3 months). Confirmed fracture: about 120 days (about 4 months). Long-standing: about 150 days (about 5 months). Move on by meeting each stage's checks, not by the calendar alone.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: Suspected? Stop sport and see a doctor for a scan before starting; Numbness or weakness in the legs; Pain at night or at rest.

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
