Calf strain: rehab exercises & recovery plan

A tear in the calf muscles (gastrocnemius or soleus), usually a sudden pull while pushing off or accelerating. Soleus strains can come on more gradually during running.

Common in: Football, running, tennis, veterans · Body area: Shin & calf · Updated November 2026

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Start the Calf strain plan

Typical signs

  • Sudden sharp pull in the calf
  • Pain rising onto the toes
  • Tightness, sometimes bruising

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • Felt like being kicked in the back of the heel, cannot stand on tiptoe (possible Achilles rupture)
  • Calf hot, red and swollen, especially after a long journey or surgery (possible blood clot)

How long does recovery take?

It depends on how serious the injury is. These are typical plan lengths:

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Days 0–7

Protect

Goal: Walk normally, gentle activation.

  1. Ankle pumps3 × 20

    Mobility · Circulation, ankle range
    1. Lie or sit with the leg straight.
    2. Pull toes up towards you, then point them away.
    3. Keep a steady rhythm.

    Tip: Moves swelling out after injury or surgery.

  2. Isometric calf hold5 × 30 s

    Isometric · Achilles isometric · pain-free
    1. Rise onto the toes halfway on one or two legs.
    2. Hold the position.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Helps settle Achilles pain before heavier loading.

  3. Stationary bike10 min

    Cardio · Knee range, fitness
    1. Seat high enough that the knee is almost straight at the bottom.
    2. Low resistance.
    3. Steady pedalling.

    Tip: Rock back and forth first if a full turn is not yet possible.

  4. Glute bridge3 × 12

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
    2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips into a straight line.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Push through heels; don't arch the low back.

Move on when:

  • Walking without limp
  • Two-leg tiptoe pain-free
Stage 2 · Weeks 1–3

Strength

Goal: Rebuild calf strength.

  1. Calf raise3 × 15

    Strength · Calves
    1. Stand on both feet, hold a support.
    2. Rise high onto the balls of the feet.
    3. Lower slowly over 3 s.

    Tip: Move to the edge of a step for more range.

  2. Bent-knee calf raise3 × 15

    Strength · Soleus
    1. Stand with knees bent about 30° (or seated with weight on the knees).
    2. Rise onto the toes keeping the knee bend.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: The soleus takes huge load when running.

  3. Single-leg calf raise3 × 10

    Strength · Calves, Achilles
    1. Stand on one foot, light hand support.
    2. Rise as high as possible.
    3. Lower over 3 s.

    Tip: Healthy target: 25+ reps each side.

  4. Toe walking3 × 1

    Strength · Calves, ankle stability · 20 m
    1. Rise high onto the balls of your feet.
    2. Walk forward without letting the heels drop.
    3. Walk 10–20 m.

    Tip: Keep the ankles straight, not rolling out.

  5. Single-leg balance3 × 30 s

    Balance · Balance, ankle reflexes
    1. Stand on one leg, knee soft.
    2. Progress: eyes closed → on a pillow → catching a ball.
    3. Stay tall.

    Tip: Balance training reduces repeat ankle sprains.

Move on when:

  • 20 single-leg calf raises
Stage 3 · Weeks 3–5

Spring

Goal: Bounce and jog.

  1. Pogo hops3 × 20

    Plyometric · Ankle stiffness, tendon spring
    1. Small quick hops on both feet, knees nearly straight.
    2. Stay on the balls of the feet.
    3. Land quietly.

    Tip: Progress to single-leg pogos.

  2. Skipping (A-skips)3 × 20

    Cardio · Running coordination, calf spring
    1. Skip forward driving one knee up.
    2. Land on the ball of the foot.
    3. Swing the arms opposite to the legs.

    Tip: Great warm-up before running and sprinting.

  3. Walk–jog intervals20 min

    Cardio · Return to running
    1. Start: 1 min jog / 1 min walk × 10.
    2. Each pain-free session, add jog time.
    3. Target 20–30 min continuous easy running.

    Tip: Run on flat, even ground first.

  4. Single-leg calf raise3 × 12

    Strength · Calves, Achilles · add weight
    1. Stand on one foot, light hand support.
    2. Rise as high as possible.
    3. Lower over 3 s.

    Tip: Healthy target: 25+ reps each side.

Move on when:

  • Run 20 min pain-free
Stage 4 · Week 5+

Return to sport

Goal: Sprint and change direction.

  1. Running strides6 × 1

    Cardio · Speed, hamstring loading
    1. Run 60–80 m, building up gradually.
    2. Reach the target speed in the middle third.
    3. Walk back to recover.

    Tip: Progress 60% → 75% → 90% → 100% effort across sessions.

  2. Single-leg lateral hop3 × 8

    Plyometric · Ankle & knee control
    1. Stand on one leg.
    2. Hop sideways over a line and land softly.
    3. Hop back. Stick each landing.

    Tip: Knee stays over the foot.

  3. Agility & cutting drills4 × 20 s

    Plyometric · Sport-specific movement
    1. Ladder drills, then cone shuttles.
    2. Planned cuts at 45°, later 90°.
    3. Finally unplanned cuts reacting to a partner's signal.

    Tip: Unplanned cutting is the last step before full training.

  4. Single-leg calf raise3 × 15

    Strength · Calves, Achilles · keep 2×/week
    1. Stand on one foot, light hand support.
    2. Rise as high as possible.
    3. Lower over 3 s.

    Tip: Healthy target: 25+ reps each side.

Move on when:

  • 25+ single-leg calf raises
  • Full sprint and training pain-free

Important: Calf strains re-occur often. Before sport you should do 25+ single-leg calf raises and run without pain.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Moderate

  • Progressive calf loading and criteria-based return (strength and running tests, not just time) are recommended to reduce the high re-injury rate.
  • Evidence comes mainly from cohort studies and expert consensus in elite sport.

Sources: Sports medicine reviews and consensus on calf muscle injuries

Common questions

How long does calf strain take to heal?

It depends on severity. Mild: about 30 days (about 4 weeks). Moderate: about 60 days (about 9 weeks). Severe: about 90 days (about 3 months). Move on by meeting each stage's checks, not by the calendar alone.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: Felt like being kicked in the back of the heel, cannot stand on tiptoe (possible Achilles rupture); Calf hot, red and swollen, especially after a long journey or surgery (possible blood clot).

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
