Hip-related groin pain (impingement / labral tear): rehab exercises & recovery plan

Pain from inside the hip joint, such as hip impingement (FAI) or a tear of the labrum — the cartilage ring around the socket. Felt deep in the groin.

Common in: Football, hockey, dance, martial arts · Body area: Hip & groin · Updated November 2026

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Understand the injury

Groin pain has 5 main types. Doctors group it by where it comes from (Doha agreement). Pick the one that matches where it hurts:

Adductor-relatedIliopsoas-relatedInguinal-relatedPubic-relatedHip-related

What's injured

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint. The labrum is a ring of cartilage around the socket. Hip impingement (FAI) is when the ball and socket pinch at the end of movement, which can irritate or tear the labrum.

How it happens

Deep squatting, twisting and kicking, often in people whose hip shape is naturally a bit different. Pain is deep in the groin and may click or catch.

How serious can it be?

Irritated hip

Deep groin ache after sport or long sitting.

Labral tear

Clicking, catching, pain in deep squats or crossing the legs.

After hip arthroscopy

Follow your surgeon's limits — a slower plan.

How it heals

A 3-month physio-led programme improves pain and function for many people; some need keyhole surgery. Strength and control around the hip reduce pinching.

Muscles to strengthen — recover and come back stronger

  • Gluteus medius (side hip)Controls the hip so the ball doesn't drift into the pinching position.
  • GlutesStrong glutes take load away from the front of the hip.
  • Core (abs & obliques)Pelvic control changes the hip angle in sport.
  • Adductors (inner thigh)Balance the inner thigh with the side hip.

Come back stronger

  • Avoid very deep squats and long sitting with knees above hips while it is sore.
  • Choose a slightly wider stance and turned-out toes for squats.
  • A specialist should check hips that keep locking or catching.

Typical signs

  • Deep groin pain, sometimes in a 'C' shape around the hip
  • Clicking, catching or locking in the hip
  • Pain in deep squats, crossing the legs or long sitting

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • Hip locks and you cannot move it
  • Teenager with hip or knee pain and a limp (needs an X-ray)
  • Fever, or severe night pain

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Weeks 0–2

Calm the joint

Goal: Pain-free hip control without deep bending.

  1. Glute bridge3 × 12

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
    2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips into a straight line.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Push through heels; don't arch the low back.

  2. Clamshell3 × 15

    Strength · Glute medius
    1. Lie on your side, knees bent, heels together.
    2. Open the top knee without rolling the pelvis back.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Add a band above the knees for progression.

  3. Side-lying hip abduction3 × 12

    Strength · Glute medius
    1. Lie on your side, bottom knee bent.
    2. Lift the straight top leg slightly behind the body.
    3. Lower slowly, toes pointing forward.

    Tip: Strong hip abductors reduce knee 'caving in'.

  4. Dead bug3 × 8

    Core · Deep core control
    1. On your back, arms up, hips and knees at 90°.
    2. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly.
    3. Keep the low back gently against the floor.

    Tip: Breathe out as the limbs lower.

  5. Stationary bike15 min

    Cardio · Knee range, fitness · seat high
    1. Seat high enough that the knee is almost straight at the bottom.
    2. Low resistance.
    3. Steady pedalling.

    Tip: Rock back and forth first if a full turn is not yet possible.

Move on when:

  • Walking and stairs pain-free
  • Sitting 30 min comfortably
Stage 2 · Weeks 2–8

Strength

Goal: Side-hip, glute and core strength.

  1. Single-leg bridge3 × 10

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Bridge position, lift one foot off the floor.
    2. Raise hips level, not twisting.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Hips stay level like a table.

  2. Band side walk3 × 12

    Strength · Glute medius
    1. Band around the knees or ankles, knees slightly bent.
    2. Step sideways, keeping tension on the band.
    3. 10 steps one way, then back.

    Tip: Toes point forward; don't let the knees cave in.

  3. Split squat3 × 8

    Strength · Quads, glutes, balance
    1. Long stride stance, back heel up.
    2. Lower the back knee straight down towards the floor.
    3. Drive up through the front heel.

    Tip: Front knee may go over the toes; keep it in line with the foot.

  4. Single-leg Romanian deadlift3 × 8

    Strength · Hamstrings, glutes, balance
    1. Stand on one leg with a soft knee.
    2. Hinge forward, the free leg going back, back flat.
    3. Return to standing by squeezing the glute.

    Tip: Hold a wall at first. Hips stay square to the floor.

  5. Side plank3 × 30 s

    Core · Obliques, side of the trunk
    1. On your side, elbow under the shoulder.
    2. Knees bent first, then straight legs.
    3. Lift hips into a line and hold.

    Tip: Head, hips, feet in one line.

  6. Pallof press3 × 10

    Core · Core anti-rotation
    1. Stand side-on to a band anchored at chest height.
    2. Hold the band at your chest, then press it straight out.
    3. Hold 2 s without twisting, bring it back.

    Tip: The trunk must not rotate towards the anchor.

Move on when:

  • Single-leg squat to a box with good control
  • No clicking pain in daily life
Stage 3 · Weeks 8+

Return to play

Goal: Running, turning and kicking.

  1. Single-leg squat to box3 × 8

    Strength · Quads, glutes, knee control
    1. Stand on one leg in front of a box or chair.
    2. Slowly sit back until you lightly touch the box.
    3. Stand up on the same leg, knee in line with the 2nd toe.

    Tip: Use a higher box to make it easier.

  2. Single-leg lateral hop3 × 8

    Plyometric · Ankle & knee control
    1. Stand on one leg.
    2. Hop sideways over a line and land softly.
    3. Hop back. Stick each landing.

    Tip: Knee stays over the foot.

  3. Walk–jog intervals20 min

    Cardio · Return to running
    1. Start: 1 min jog / 1 min walk × 10.
    2. Each pain-free session, add jog time.
    3. Target 20–30 min continuous easy running.

    Tip: Run on flat, even ground first.

  4. Agility & cutting drills4 × 20 s

    Plyometric · Sport-specific movement
    1. Ladder drills, then cone shuttles.
    2. Planned cuts at 45°, later 90°.
    3. Finally unplanned cuts reacting to a partner's signal.

    Tip: Unplanned cutting is the last step before full training.

  5. Running strides6 × 1

    Cardio · Speed, hamstring loading
    1. Run 60–80 m, building up gradually.
    2. Reach the target speed in the middle third.
    3. Walk back to recover.

    Tip: Progress 60% → 75% → 90% → 100% effort across sessions.

Move on when:

  • Full training without pain the next day

Important: While it is sore, avoid very deep squats and long sitting with knees higher than hips. Use a slightly wider stance with toes turned out.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Moderate

  • In a large trial of hip impingement, both keyhole surgery and a physio-led exercise programme improved pain and function; surgery gave a slightly bigger improvement at 12 months.
  • Strengthening the hip and core is the first step for most people.

Sources: Griffin et al. 2018, The Lancet (UK FASHIoN trial, 348 patients)

Common questions

How long does hip-related groin pain take to heal?

It depends on severity. Irritated hip: about 56 days (about 8 weeks). Labral tear: about 84 days (about 2 months). After hip arthroscopy: about 140 days (about 4 months). Move on by meeting each stage's checks, not by the calendar alone.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: Hip locks and you cannot move it; Teenager with hip or knee pain and a limp (needs an X-ray); Fever, or severe night pain.

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
