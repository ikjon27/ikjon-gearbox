Upper back & posture pain: rehab exercises & recovery plan

An ache or stiffness between the shoulder blades, often from long sitting, phone use or heavy training. It is usually muscular and improves with movement and strengthening the upper back.

Common in: Students, desk work, gym · Body area: Back & core · Updated November 2026

Written by: IKJON teamSources: 1 studies and guidelinesLast checked: 9 November 2026How we write health pages →

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Understand the injury

What's injured

Between the shoulder blades sit the rhomboids and the middle and lower trapezius. With long sitting they become tired and sore, and the upper back stiffens. Nothing is damaged.

How it happens

Hours of sitting, phone use or driving with rounded shoulders, plus little upper-body strength training.

How serious can it be?

Mild

Ache after long sitting.

Persistent

Pain most days for weeks.

Long-standing

Months of ache — strength and movement breaks help most.

How it heals

Moving the stiff upper back and strengthening the muscles between the shoulder blades usually helps within weeks.

Muscles to strengthen — recover and come back stronger

  • Upper backRhomboids and trapezius — rows and Y-raises.
  • Rotator cuffShoulder control for good arm position.
  • Core (abs & obliques)Supports an upright posture without effort.
  • ChestStretch and balance tight chest muscles.

Come back stronger

  • Change position every 30–45 minutes; no single posture is perfect.
  • Do rows or band pull-aparts 3 times a week.
  • Set your screen at eye height.

Typical signs

  • Ache between the shoulder blades
  • Stiff after long sitting
  • Feels better when moving

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • Chest pain with breathlessness, sweating or pain into the arm or jaw — call an ambulance
  • Pain after a fall or accident
  • Fever, night pain or unexplained weight loss

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Weeks 0–2

Move

Goal: Restore upper-back movement.

  1. Open book2 × 8

    Mobility · Upper back rotation
    1. Lie on your side, knees bent, arms together in front.
    2. Open the top arm across to the other side, following it with your eyes.
    3. Return slowly.

    Tip: Breathe out as you open.

  2. Seated upper-back extension2 × 10

    Mobility · Upper back mobility
    1. Sit on a chair, hands behind your head.
    2. Gently arch the upper back over the chair back, looking up.
    3. Return to upright.

    Tip: Move from the upper back, not the low back.

  3. Cat–camel2 × 8

    Mobility · Spine mobility
    1. On hands and knees.
    2. Slowly round the back up, then let it sag gently.
    3. Move within comfort.

    Tip: Mobility, not stretching. Keep it gentle.

  4. Chin tuck3 × 10

    Isometric · Deep neck flexors
    1. Sit or stand tall, eyes level.
    2. Glide the chin straight back (make a 'double chin').
    3. Hold 5 s, relax.

    Tip: Don't nod down — slide straight back.

  5. Child's pose2 × 30 s

    Stretch · Back, hips
    1. Kneel and sit back towards your heels.
    2. Reach the arms forward on the floor.
    3. Relax and breathe into the back.

    Tip: A cushion between heels and bottom helps if knees are stiff.

Move on when:

  • Sitting 1 hour with little ache
Stage 2 · Weeks 2–5

Strengthen

Goal: Upper-back and shoulder-blade strength.

  1. Bent-over row3 × 12

    Strength · Upper back, shoulder blades
    1. Hinge forward with a flat back, weights or band in hands.
    2. Pull the elbows back, squeezing the shoulder blades.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Keep the neck long and the back flat.

  2. Prone T & Y raise3 × 10

    Strength · Lower & middle trapezius
    1. Lie face down on a bench or the floor.
    2. Lift the arms out to a 'T', then up to a 'Y', thumbs up.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Small lift, no shrugging.

  3. Band pull-apart3 × 15

    Strength · Rear shoulder, upper back
    1. Hold a band at shoulder height, arms straight.
    2. Pull the band apart to touch the chest.
    3. Return slowly.

    Tip: Ribs down, don't arch the back.

  4. Wall slide3 × 10

    Mobility · Overhead control
    1. Forearms on a wall, elbows at shoulder height.
    2. Slide arms up the wall in a pain-free range.
    3. Slide back down slowly.

    Tip: Stop below the point of pain.

  5. Superman3 × 8

    Core · Back extensors, glutes
    1. Lie face down, arms overhead.
    2. Lift arms, chest and legs slightly off the floor.
    3. Hold 3 s, lower slowly.

    Tip: Small lift is enough — no cranking the neck.

Move on when:

  • Pain-free daily life
Stage 3 · Week 5+

Stay strong

Goal: Keep it for life.

  1. Bent-over row3 × 15

    Strength · Upper back, shoulder blades
    1. Hinge forward with a flat back, weights or band in hands.
    2. Pull the elbows back, squeezing the shoulder blades.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Keep the neck long and the back flat.

  2. Push-up3 × 10

    Strength · Chest, arms, core
    1. Hands under shoulders, body in a straight line (knees down to make it easier).
    2. Lower the chest towards the floor.
    3. Push back up without the hips sagging.

    Tip: Too hard? Start with wall push-ups.

  3. Forearm plank3 × 30 s

    Core · Whole core
    1. Forearms on the floor, elbows under shoulders.
    2. Lift into a straight line from head to heels.
    3. Hold and breathe.

    Tip: Squeeze glutes; don't let the hips sag.

  4. Prone T & Y raise3 × 12

    Strength · Lower & middle trapezius
    1. Lie face down on a bench or the floor.
    2. Lift the arms out to a 'T', then up to a 'Y', thumbs up.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Small lift, no shrugging.

  5. Doorway chest stretch2 × 30 s

    Stretch · Chest, front of shoulder
    1. Forearm on a door frame, elbow at shoulder height.
    2. Step through gently until you feel the chest stretch.
    3. Hold, then switch sides.

    Tip: Undoes rounded 'phone posture'.

Move on when:

  • Keep strength work 2×/week

Important: Change position every 30–45 minutes. Strength work for the upper back matters more than 'perfect posture'.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Limited

  • There is little research specifically on upper-back pain. This plan uses the same approach that helps neck pain: mobility plus strengthening of the upper back and shoulder blades.

Sources: Gross et al. 2015, Cochrane review (neck exercise) — applied by extension

Common questions

Can bad posture be fixed?

You can change how you sit and stand, and stronger upper-back muscles make an upright position easier to hold. Moving often matters more than finding one perfect posture.

How long does it take to improve posture?

Many people feel a difference within 4–6 weeks of regular upper-back exercises and more movement breaks. Muscles keep getting stronger over the following months.

Why does my upper back hurt between the shoulder blades?

Long periods sitting or looking at a screen load the muscles between the shoulder blades. Movement breaks, a higher screen and strengthening exercises usually help.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: Chest pain with breathlessness, sweating or pain into the arm or jaw — call an ambulance; Pain after a fall or accident; Fever, night pain or unexplained weight loss.

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
