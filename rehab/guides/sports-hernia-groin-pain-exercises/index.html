Inguinal-related groin pain (sports hernia): rehab exercises & recovery plan

Overload of the lower belly wall just above the groin crease, near the inguinal canal. Common in kicking sports. Usually there is no lump.

Common in: Football, hockey, rugby · Body area: Hip & groin · Updated November 2026

Education, not diagnosis. This guide explains how this injury is usually rehabilitated. It cannot tell you what injury you have — see a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm it, especially after a fall, a pop or surgery.

Understand the injury

Groin pain has 5 main types. Doctors group it by where it comes from (Doha agreement). Pick the one that matches where it hurts:

Adductor-relatedIliopsoas-relatedInguinal-relatedPubic-relatedHip-related

What's injured

The inguinal canal is a natural passage through the lower belly wall, just above the crease of the groin. In 'inguinal-related groin pain' (sometimes called a sports hernia) the muscles and tissues around it are overloaded — usually there is no lump.

How it happens

Repeated kicking, sprinting and twisting, often with a weak or tired core. Pain often comes on slowly.

How serious can it be?

Mild

Pain only after hard training.

Moderate

Pain during sprinting, kicking or sit-ups; coughing or sneezing may hurt.

Long-standing

Pain for months. Most improve with 6–12 weeks of exercise; a few need a surgeon's opinion.

How it heals

Most athletes recover with a programme that strengthens the core and hips and improves movement control — in one large study players returned to sport after about 10 weeks of rehab.

Muscles to strengthen — recover and come back stronger

  • Core (abs & obliques)The key area — deep abs and obliques protect the lower belly wall.
  • Adductors (inner thigh)Often weak at the same time; balance them with the core.
  • GlutesControl the pelvis when you kick and change direction.
  • Hip flexorsTrain them without overloading the groin.

Come back stronger

  • A visible or hard lump in the groin is a hernia — see a doctor.
  • Train the core with control exercises (dead bug, Pallof press) before crunches.
  • Increase kicking distance and sprint speed step by step.

Typical signs

  • Pain in the lower belly or groin crease
  • Worse with sprinting, kicking or sit-ups
  • Coughing or sneezing may hurt

See a doctor first if you have any of these

  • A lump in the groin, especially one that gets bigger when you cough (hernia)
  • Pain in the testicles, or pain passing urine
  • Fever or feeling unwell

The rehab plan, stage by stage

Move to the next stage only when you meet its “move on when” checks — not just because the weeks have passed.

Stage 1 · Weeks 0–2

Settle & core control

Goal: Calm symptoms; train deep core control.

  1. Dead bug3 × 8

    Core · Deep core control
    1. On your back, arms up, hips and knees at 90°.
    2. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly.
    3. Keep the low back gently against the floor.

    Tip: Breathe out as the limbs lower.

  2. Pallof press3 × 10

    Core · Core anti-rotation
    1. Stand side-on to a band anchored at chest height.
    2. Hold the band at your chest, then press it straight out.
    3. Hold 2 s without twisting, bring it back.

    Tip: The trunk must not rotate towards the anchor.

  3. Glute bridge3 × 12

    Strength · Glutes, hamstrings
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat.
    2. Squeeze glutes and lift hips into a straight line.
    3. Lower slowly.

    Tip: Push through heels; don't arch the low back.

  4. Adductor squeeze4 × 10 s

    Isometric · Groin isometric · medium effort
    1. Lie on your back, knees bent, ball or fist between the knees.
    2. Squeeze at a comfortable effort.
    3. Hold, then relax.

    Tip: Increase squeeze effort as pain allows.

  5. Stationary bike15 min

    Cardio · Knee range, fitness
    1. Seat high enough that the knee is almost straight at the bottom.
    2. Low resistance.
    3. Steady pedalling.

    Tip: Rock back and forth first if a full turn is not yet possible.

Move on when:

  • Daily life and cycling pain-free
  • Coughing pain-free
Stage 2 · Weeks 2–6

Strength & control

Goal: Hip, groin and trunk strength together.

  1. Copenhagen plank3 × 15 s

    Strength · Adductors (groin) · short lever
    1. Side plank with the top leg on a bench.
    2. Short lever: knee on the bench. Long lever: ankle on the bench.
    3. Lift hips and hold, bottom leg free.

    Tip: Shown to cut groin injuries in football by around 40%.

  2. Lateral lunge3 × 8

    Strength · Adductors, glutes
    1. Wide stance.
    2. Shift to one side, bending that knee, other leg straight.
    3. Push back to centre.

    Tip: Keep the bent knee in line with the foot.

  3. McGill curl-up3 × 8

    Core · Abdominals with spine protection
    1. On your back, one knee bent, hands under the low back.
    2. Lift head and shoulders just off the floor, neck still.
    3. Hold 8–10 s.

    Tip: The low back stays in its natural curve.

  4. Bird dog3 × 8

    Core · Back extensors, core
    1. On hands and knees.
    2. Reach opposite arm and leg out long.
    3. Hold 5–10 s, don't rotate the hips.

    Tip: Imagine a cup of tea on your lower back.

  5. Single-leg Romanian deadlift3 × 8

    Strength · Hamstrings, glutes, balance
    1. Stand on one leg with a soft knee.
    2. Hinge forward, the free leg going back, back flat.
    3. Return to standing by squeezing the glute.

    Tip: Hold a wall at first. Hips stay square to the floor.

  6. Side plank3 × 30 s

    Core · Obliques, side of the trunk
    1. On your side, elbow under the shoulder.
    2. Knees bent first, then straight legs.
    3. Lift hips into a line and hold.

    Tip: Head, hips, feet in one line.

Move on when:

  • Lateral lunge and Copenhagen pain-free
  • Sit-ups pain-free
Stage 3 · Weeks 6+

Running & cutting

Goal: Back to sprinting, kicking and turning.

  1. Walk–jog intervals20 min

    Cardio · Return to running
    1. Start: 1 min jog / 1 min walk × 10.
    2. Each pain-free session, add jog time.
    3. Target 20–30 min continuous easy running.

    Tip: Run on flat, even ground first.

  2. Running strides6 × 1

    Cardio · Speed, hamstring loading
    1. Run 60–80 m, building up gradually.
    2. Reach the target speed in the middle third.
    3. Walk back to recover.

    Tip: Progress 60% → 75% → 90% → 100% effort across sessions.

  3. Lateral bounds3 × 8

    Plyometric · Side-to-side power & control
    1. Jump sideways from one leg to the other.
    2. Land and stick for 2 s before the next.
    3. Increase distance gradually.

    Tip: Knee must not cave inward on landing.

  4. Agility & cutting drills4 × 20 s

    Plyometric · Sport-specific movement
    1. Ladder drills, then cone shuttles.
    2. Planned cuts at 45°, later 90°.
    3. Finally unplanned cuts reacting to a partner's signal.

    Tip: Unplanned cutting is the last step before full training.

  5. Copenhagen plank3 × 20 s

    Strength · Adductors (groin) · long lever
    1. Side plank with the top leg on a bench.
    2. Short lever: knee on the bench. Long lever: ankle on the bench.
    3. Lift hips and hold, bottom leg free.

    Tip: Shown to cut groin injuries in football by around 40%.

Move on when:

  • Full training and long kicks pain-free

Important: Most athletes recover with exercise in 6–12 weeks. If pain continues after a good programme, ask a sports doctor or surgeon.

What the research says

Strength of evidence: Moderate

  • In 205 athletes with groin pain, a rehab programme targeting strength and movement control let most return to play after about 10 weeks.
  • Surgery is considered when a good exercise programme has not worked.

Sources: King et al. 2018, British Journal of Sports Medicine (cohort, 205 athletes); Weir et al. 2015, BJSM (Doha agreement)

Common questions

How long does inguinal-related groin pain take to heal?

It depends on severity. Mild: about 42 days (about 6 weeks). Moderate: about 70 days (about 10 weeks). Long-standing: about 112 days (about 3 months). Move on by meeting each stage's checks, not by the calendar alone.

When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor or physiotherapist to confirm the injury, and get help urgently if you notice: A lump in the groin, especially one that gets bigger when you cough (hernia); Pain in the testicles, or pain passing urine; Fever or feeling unwell.

Should exercises hurt?

Mild discomfort up to about 3–4 out of 10 that settles within 24 hours is usually acceptable. Stop if pain is sharp, goes above 5 out of 10, or swelling increases afterwards.
